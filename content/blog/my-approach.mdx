<Block title="How I learn">
When I started Flutter, I was new to almost every concept and my code broke at every step. It was frustrating, but I noticed that whenever it broke, I could hand the error to my code agent and get it fixed. Each fix showed me what I'd gotten wrong, and over time the breaks got fewer. I learn through real projects like this: I build something, let it break, and treat every fix as a lesson. Claude, Gemini and ChatGPT are my tutors along the way.

I don't just accept the fix — I ask why it broke and make sure I understand the underlying pattern. That keeps me learning and fits right into my core philosophy of *Learning to Build With AI Without Losing the Thread*.
</Block>

<Block title="How I build">
I used to think of myself as someone who just builds projects. Pitching TalentBridge CM at the NAHPI Hackathon changed that. A project is only worth something if it solves a real problem for real people, and if it can generate revenue to sustain itself.

Building something that works technically isn't enough — it has to matter to someone enough that they would pay for it. I design products problem-first: defining who has the problem, how the solution fixes it, and how the business model sustains its growth.
</Block>

<Block title="How I iterate">
I believe in rapid validation using real local context and infrastructure. Whether it's integrating NotchPay sandbox payments for Mobile Money (MTN MoMo, Orange Money) in Cameroon or building bilingual matching algorithms, I test features directly in real-world scenarios.

I ship early MVPs, gather user feedback, and refine the architecture continuously — turning early prototypes into reliable production applications.
</Block>
